# Style 02 — Scandinavian Luxury

## Design Identity

Refined Scandinavian interior with a soft luxury direction.

The design should feel bright, airy, calm, warm and sophisticated.

Use Scandinavian simplicity as the foundation, elevated through premium materials, refined furniture and subtle luxury details.

Avoid making the room look rustic, overly minimalist or inexpensive.

## Colour Palette

Primary:
- Warm white
- Soft cream
- Pale beige
- Light grey

Secondary:
- Natural oak
- Light ash
- Warm greige

Accent:
- Muted black
- Soft taupe
- Restrained brushed brass

Keep the palette light and cohesive.

## Materials

Preferred:
- Light oak
- Ash timber
- Natural stone
- Quartz
- Linen
- Boucle
- Wool
- Matte plaster
- Light ceramic
- Clear glass
- Brushed metal

Timber should feel natural rather than heavily orange or yellow.

## Furniture Language

Use:
- Soft curved sofas
- Light timber furniture
- Rounded coffee tables
- Simple dining tables
- Sculptural Scandinavian chairs
- Upholstered accent chairs
- Minimal cabinetry
- Soft upholstered beds

Furniture should have elegant proportions and visible craftsmanship.

## Lighting

Use:
- Abundant natural daylight
- Soft diffused window light
- Minimal recessed lighting
- Scandinavian pendant fixtures
- Soft warm floor lamps
- Subtle wall lighting

Keep lighting gentle and comfortable.

## Curtains

Prefer:
- White or warm ivory sheers
- Linen
- Floor-to-ceiling curtains
- Soft neutral fabrics

## Decorative Styling

Use:
- Minimal artwork
- Ceramics
- Small plants
- Natural branches
- Books
- Soft textiles
- Sculptural decorative pieces

Decor should reinforce the calm Scandinavian character.

## Spatial Character

The space should feel:
- Bright
- Airy
- Calm
- Elegant
- Comfortable
- Premium
- Understated

## Avoid

- Heavy dark timber
- Excessive black
- Industrial styling
- Rustic farmhouse elements
- Strong colours
- Excessive decoration
- Cold clinical white
- Cheap-looking furniture
- Overly ornate luxury

## Photorealism Direction

Use soft daylight, realistic fabric texture, natural timber grain, believable stone surfaces and subtle reflections.

The result should resemble a premium Scandinavian residential interior photographed by an architectural photographer.
