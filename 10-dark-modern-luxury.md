# Style 10 — Dark Modern Luxury

## Design Identity

Sophisticated contemporary interior using darker materials, warm lighting and strong tonal contrast.

The design should feel dramatic and luxurious without becoming theatrical or overly masculine.

Maintain residential comfort and visual balance.

## Colour Palette

Primary:
- Charcoal
- Deep taupe
- Espresso
- Warm grey

Secondary:
- Dark walnut
- Deep brown
- Warm stone

Contrast:
- Cream
- Warm white
- Soft beige

Accent:
- Brushed bronze
- Muted brass
- Black

## Materials

Preferred:
- Dark walnut
- Smoked oak
- Dark natural stone
- Travertine used for contrast
- Marble used selectively
- Smoked glass
- Clear glass
- Brushed bronze
- Leather used selectively
- Velvet used sparingly
- Premium woven fabrics

## Furniture Language

Use:
- Low-profile sofas
- Deep comfortable seating
- Sculptural lounge chairs
- Dark timber furniture
- Stone coffee tables
- Premium upholstered furniture
- Refined dining furniture

Use lighter furniture or textiles where necessary to prevent the room becoming visually heavy.

## Lighting

Lighting is critical.

Use:
- Warm indirect lighting
- Recessed downlights
- Cove lighting
- Wall lighting
- Decorative table lamps
- Statement pendants
- Soft natural daylight

Avoid excessively bright cool-white lighting.

## Curtains

Prefer:
- Warm taupe
- Charcoal
- Deep beige
- Heavy linen
- Soft textured fabrics

Use sheers where needed to maintain daylight.

## Decorative Styling

Use:
- Large restrained artwork
- Sculptural objects
- Dark ceramics
- Books
- Minimal greenery
- Premium decorative objects

Keep decoration controlled.

## Spatial Character

The room should feel:
- Sophisticated
- Dramatic
- Premium
- Intimate
- Elegant
- Contemporary
- Comfortable

## Avoid

- Excessive black
- Gothic appearance
- Excessive gold
- Overly dark rooms
- Cold lighting
- Excessive marble
- Excessive glossy surfaces
- Excessive leather
- Corporate office appearance
- Nightclub appearance

## Photorealism Direction

Dark materials must retain visible texture and detail.

Do not crush shadows into black.

Maintain believable tonal range, material reflections and subtle surface variation.

Warm lighting should illuminate furniture and architectural surfaces naturally.

The result should resemble a professionally photographed high-end contemporary residence rather than a cinematic CGI scene.
