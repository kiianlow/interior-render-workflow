# Style 03 — Japandi Warm Minimalism

## Design Identity

A refined combination of Japanese simplicity and Scandinavian warmth.

The space should feel calm, intentional, tactile and naturally luxurious.

Prioritise simplicity, negative space, natural materials and carefully selected furniture.

## Colour Palette

Primary:
- Warm ivory
- Sand
- Beige
- Taupe

Secondary:
- Natural oak
- Medium warm timber
- Clay
- Soft stone

Accent:
- Charcoal
- Muted brown
- Very restrained black

## Materials

Preferred:
- Natural oak
- Walnut accents
- Travertine
- Limestone
- Microcement
- Limewash
- Natural plaster
- Linen
- Cotton
- Woven textures
- Handmade ceramics
- Matte timber

Materials should look tactile and authentic.

## Furniture Language

Use:
- Low-profile sofas
- Low timber furniture
- Rounded coffee tables
- Simple dining tables
- Minimal wooden chairs
- Platform-style beds
- Simple shelving
- Organic-shaped furniture

Furniture should feel quiet rather than visually dominant.

## Lighting

Use:
- Soft natural daylight
- Warm indirect lighting
- Paper-like or fabric pendant fixtures
- Subtle recessed lights
- Warm floor lamps

Avoid harsh direct lighting.

## Curtains

Prefer:
- Natural linen
- Warm white
- Beige
- Soft taupe
- Simple full-height curtains

## Decorative Styling

Use very little decoration.

Suitable:
- Handmade ceramics
- Stone objects
- Small branches
- Minimal artwork
- Natural plants
- Wooden objects
- Woven baskets

Every decorative object should feel intentional.

## Spatial Character

The room should feel:
- Peaceful
- Warm
- Minimal
- Natural
- Tactile
- Sophisticated
- Balanced

## Avoid

- Excessive decoration
- Bright colours
- Glossy surfaces
- Excessive metallic finishes
- Ornate furniture
- Visual clutter
- Industrial elements
- Strong luxury ornamentation

## Photorealism Direction

Emphasise natural imperfections in timber, stone, plaster and ceramics.

Use soft daylight, realistic material roughness and subtle shadows.

The final result should feel like a high-end Japanese-Scandinavian residential interior rather than a stylised showroom.
